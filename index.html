    1. 01 / 07 · Multi-agent LLM system

      Autonomous farm inspection planner

      A season inspection plan for 40+ farms took three days to create. I designed and built five LangGraph agents for planning, scheduling, search, verification and optimisation that produce them from weather, sensor and harvest data. 65% of routine scheduling is now automated.

    2. 02 / 07 · Computer vision, vision-language models

      AgriVision: language-guided crop segmentation

      Drone images could only be searched through 12 fixed categories. I fine-tuned SAM 3 and LISA with LoRA so people can ask in plain language, such as "all diseased plants, worst first", and get ranked instance masks back. Three legacy pipelines were retired.

      • A redesigned ranking head scores several instances per query; queries are answered 2x faster with 40% less preprocessing.
      • Runs on low-power field hardware through ONNX and TensorRT with two custom C++ plugins on Triton; per-GPU throughput tripled.
      • Stable Diffusion synthetic data and YOLO-assisted pre-labelling for disease classes with fewer than 200 labelled images.

      PyTorch · SAM 3 · LISA · CLIP · LLaVA · LoRA/PEFT · Stable Diffusion · Detectron2 · YOLO · ONNX · TensorRT · Triton · C++ · Kubernetes

    3. 03 / 07 · Research, master's thesis

      Text-prompted instance segmentation with SAM 3

      My master's thesis fine-tunes SAM 3 (Segment Anything Model 3) to segment individual plants in agricultural images from a text prompt alone, with no boxes or clicks to help the model.

      • The hard part is language about relations and absence: which plant is "leftmost", which are "the largest", and when the right answer is no mask at all.
      • Lab 01 further down this page is a small classical stand-in for the same task.

    4. 04 / 07 · Inference, distributed training, quantization

      Benchmarking and shrinking open LLMs

      Which serving stack should a research group standardise on, and how far can limited GPUs be pushed? I built a reproducible benchmark of vLLM, TGI, SGLang and Hugging Face generation on 7B to 13B models under varying load and memory pressure. vLLM became the group's default.

          Lab 05 · LLM serving
          
      
          Why continuous batching wins
          
      
          Two copies of one GPU with eight slots receive the same stream of requests. Static batching holds finished slots until the whole batch is done. Continuous batching refills a slot the moment it frees up.
          
      
          Raise the arrival rate and watch the top queue grow while the bottom one keeps up.
          
      
          3.4 s → 1.1 sresponse latency in production, using continuous batching, KV-cache reuse and speculative decoding on vLLM.
